# Chapter 1: The Shop on Mill Lane

Nell Ashby kept the bookshop at the bottom of Mill Lane in Thornbury, and on Tuesday afternoons she walked up the lane to Silas Venn's antique shop to see what he had bought at the sales. Venn was a small dry man of sixty who sold clocks, brass and bad pictures, and he liked to make her wait.

That Tuesday he made her wait because he was quarrelling. Margaret Lyle, the vicar's sister, stood at his counter with her basket on her arm, white about the mouth.

"Forty pounds by Michaelmas, Miss Lyle," Venn said. "Or I go to your brother and tell him where the altar silver went."

"You would not dare."

"Try me."

Margaret Lyle turned and walked out past Nell without seeing her. In her basket, among the parsnips, was a green glass bottle with a cork in it, the sort chemists sell tonic in.

Venn sold Nell a box of old sermons for a shilling and would not say what the quarrel had been about.

Constable Tom Hale was leaning on the railings outside when she came out. He had been at school with Nell, and he had been in love with her since he was twelve, which everyone in Thornbury knew except, apparently, Nell.

"Venn's shouting again," he said. "I could hear him from the pump."

"He was shouting at Margaret Lyle."

Tom whistled. "Then he is braver than I am." He took the box of sermons from her and carried it down the lane. At her door he stopped and turned his helmet round in his hands.

"There's the harvest dance on Saturday," he said.

"There is," said Nell.

"Would you come to it? With me, I mean."

Nell looked at him for a long moment. "Ask me again on Friday," she said, and went in, and leaned on the inside of the door, smiling.
