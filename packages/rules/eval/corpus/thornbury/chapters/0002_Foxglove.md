# Chapter 2: Foxglove

Mrs Dunn, who kept house for Silas Venn, found him at seven the next morning, sitting at his desk with his cheek on the account book and a cold cup of tea at his elbow. She screamed once and then ran for the constable.

Tom Hale had seen dead men before, in France, but never one in a room that smelled of beeswax and clocks. Venn's lips were blue. Doctor Marsh came from the surgery in his shirtsleeves, looked at the dead man's eyes and at the teacup, and sniffed.

"Foxglove," he said. "Digitalis, enough to stop a horse. It was in his tea, or I am a Dutchman."

Tom knelt by the grate. Among the ashes lay the pieces of a green glass bottle, still smelling faintly bitter. He wrapped them in his handkerchief and put them in his pocket.

"Who would want to poison Silas Venn?" said Mrs Dunn, who knew the answer was half the town.

"Who comes into his money?" Tom asked.

"His nephew, Peter. From Hexham. He was here on Sunday asking for a loan, and Mr Venn sent him off with a flea in his ear."

Tom wrote Peter Venn's name in his notebook and underlined it twice. A nephew who needed money and an uncle who would not give it: it was the oldest story in the world, and the sergeant at Hexham would like it very much.

He locked the shop behind him and walked back down Mill Lane. The bookshop's blind was still down. He did not knock. He told himself it was because it was early, and knew that it was not.
