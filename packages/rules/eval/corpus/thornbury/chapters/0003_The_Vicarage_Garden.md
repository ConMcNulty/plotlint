# Chapter 3: The Vicarage Garden

By noon all Thornbury knew that Silas Venn was dead, and by one o'clock it knew he had been poisoned. Nell Ashby heard it from the baker, the butcher and the postmistress, in that order, each with more detail than the last.

At three she walked up to the vicarage with the vicar's library book, which was a week overdue and gave her an excuse. The vicar was out. His sister was not.

Margaret Lyle was in the garden, on her knees by the long border under the wall, and she did not hear Nell on the path. The border had been full of foxgloves all summer, tall and purple and freckled at the throat. Now every stem was cut down to the ground, and the cuttings were gone.

"Miss Lyle," Nell said. "I've brought the book back."

Margaret Lyle stood up too quickly. Her basket was on the grass beside her. The parsnips were still in it, but the green glass bottle was not.

"Leave it on the hall table," she said. "Thank you, Miss Ashby."

Nell left it on the hall table. On her way out she passed the kitchen window and saw a saucepan on the range, and a muslin bag hung over it to drip, and she smelled something green and bitter that she had smelled once before, in her grandmother's stillroom, when she was very small.

She walked home the long way, by the churchyard, thinking. Foxgloves for the heart, her grandmother used to say: one leaf for the heart, and two leaves for the grave.
