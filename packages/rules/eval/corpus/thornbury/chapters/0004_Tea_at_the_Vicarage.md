# Chapter 4: Tea at the Vicarage

Nell went to the police house on Thursday morning and told Tom Hale everything: the quarrel, the bottle in the basket, the border cut to the ground, the muslin bag over the saucepan. He listened without writing anything down, which she knew meant he was taking it seriously.

"Peter Venn was in Hexham all Monday night," he said at last. "The sergeant there has six men who drank with him."

"Then come to tea at the vicarage," said Nell.

Margaret Lyle poured the tea herself. She did not offer them sugar. When Tom took the pieces of green glass from his handkerchief and laid them on the tablecloth, she looked at them for a long time.

"He was going to tell my brother," she said. "About the altar silver. I sold it to pay for my mother's nursing, and then I could not buy it back, and he knew. He would have told the whole county." She folded her hands. "I made the tincture from the foxgloves in the border and put it in the green bottle, and I took it to him on Monday night and poured it in his tea while he counted my money. I poisoned Silas Venn, Constable. I am not sorry."

Tom arrested her very gently, and the vicar came home to find his sister gone and two cold cups on the tray.

That evening Tom walked Nell back down Mill Lane. At her door he turned his helmet round in his hands, as he always did.

"It is Friday tomorrow," he said.

"It is."

"You said to ask you again on Friday."

"Ask me now," said Nell. "I love you, Tom Hale. I have for years. Yes, I will come to the dance, and yes to anything else you were going to ask."

He kissed her on the doorstep, in front of the whole of Mill Lane, and by morning they were engaged and all Thornbury knew that too.
