# Chapter 3: The Ledger

It was the fourth of October when they came back to Wenlow. They had walked all the third and slept in a barn, and they went straight to Crane's cottage without stopping at their own.

The parish nurse let them in. The cottage smelled of camphor and wet wool. Absalom Crane lay propped on three pillows, grey in the face, and his breath came short and loud.

"Marrow's children," he said. "I wondered which of you would come. I see it is both."

Elspeth sat on the stool by the bed. Jonas stood at the foot of it with his cap in his hands.

"You wrote the letter," Elspeth said.

"I did. I signed your father's name because I thought the warden would come for his name and not for mine." He coughed for a long time. "I ferried the Margery's crew the night she sailed, and I wrote it in the ledger, and I wrote something else in it too. Your father did not steal anything. He was paid to sink the barge and he would not, and the men who paid him sank it with him aboard."

"Who?"

"It is in the ledger. Names, dates, the price. I was a coward for ten years, Elspeth. I am too tired to be one now."

He reached under his pillow with a shaking hand and pressed the ledger and a small iron key into her hands. The ledger was heavier than it looked. The key was cold and pitted with rust.

"The strongbox is under the third flagstone of the abbey chapel on Gorse Hill," Crane said. "Your father hid it there the week before the Margery sailed, because he knew what was coming. That key opens it. He gave it to me to keep, and I have kept it."

Jonas came around the bed and knelt beside his sister. "Why didn't you tell anyone?"

"Because the men who sank the Margery are still alive, and one of them is still on the parish council." Crane closed his eyes. "Take the ledger to the warden, but take the strongbox first. Without the gold, the ledger is only an old man's word."

They sat with him through the evening. The nurse came and went. Once Crane asked for water, and once he asked for his oar, as if he meant to get up and row, and Jonas told him gently that the oar was safe in the boat.

Absalom Crane died a little before midnight, with the lamp still burning and the window open to the sound of the river.

Elspeth did not cry until they were outside. Then she cried hard, and Jonas held her, and the river went past in the dark as it always had.

When she could speak again she took the silver compass from her pocket and gave it to Jonas.

"Keep it," she said. "You will need it on the hill. I have the ledger and the key, and that is enough for me to carry."

Jonas closed his hand around the compass. "We go tomorrow?"

"We go tomorrow. Before anyone on the parish council hears that Crane is dead."
