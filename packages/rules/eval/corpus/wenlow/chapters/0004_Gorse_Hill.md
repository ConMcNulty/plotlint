# Chapter 4: Gorse Hill

On the fifth of October they set out for the abbey ruins on Gorse Hill, which was half a day's walk north of Wenlow. Before they left, Elspeth went to the smithy and bought a spade and a lantern. The smith wanted two shillings for the pair, and she paid him, which left her seven.

Jonas went ahead with the compass. The path climbed through gorse and bracken and then through nothing at all, only wind and sheep and the grey stones of field walls that had fallen down a long time ago. Twice the mist came in so thick that Elspeth could not see her brother, and twice she called and he answered from somewhere above her.

"East of true," Jonas said, tapping the compass, when she caught up with him. "Father always said so. If you know how wrong a thing is, you can still steer by it."

The abbey had been a ruin for three hundred years. Its walls stood up out of the turf like broken teeth, and the chapel at the east end had lost its roof and kept its floor. Elspeth counted the flagstones from the chapel door. The third was cracked across one corner and sat a finger's breadth lower than the others.

They took turns with the spade. The flagstone came up at last with a sound like a held breath let go. Underneath, in a hole lined with slate, was a strongbox the size of a bread oven, black with damp and bound in iron.

Elspeth knelt and fitted the iron key into the lock. It would not turn. She tried again, and Jonas lit the lantern so that she could see, and on the third try something gave and the lid came up.

Inside, wrapped in oilcloth, were the bags of the Wenlow parish money, and on top of them a letter in her father's hand.

She did not read it. She could not, not there. She put it inside her coat beside the ledger and sat back on her heels.

"Now what?" said Jonas.

A voice answered from the chapel door. "Now you give that box to me."

Captain Ida Thorne stood in the doorway with the mist behind her. She was alone, and she had walked; her boots were wet to the knee.

"You followed us," Elspeth said.

"I followed Crane's nurse, who told me where you had gone, and I followed you from the village." Thorne came forward and looked down into the hole. "Sixty pounds in gold. The parish council will be very glad, and some of them will be very frightened. You did well."

"We are taking it back to Wenlow ourselves," said Elspeth. "In daylight, where people can see."

Thorne considered that for a long moment. Then she nodded. "That is wiser than giving it to me. I will walk beside you."

Elspeth carried the strongbox down the hill herself, though it bruised her arms. Jonas carried the spade and the lantern, and Thorne walked a little behind them, watching the path and the mist and the gorse on either side, as if she expected someone to come out of it.

Nobody did. But that night, back in Wenlow, someone broke a window at the Marrows' cottage and left without taking anything, and in the morning they found boot prints in the mud under the sill.
